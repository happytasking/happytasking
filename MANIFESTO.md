# The Happy Tasking Manifesto

## Know before you task.

AI is being built by more people than most people see.

Behind models and products are thousands of developers, evaluators, reviewers, annotators, researchers, domain experts, and independent contributors. They evaluate outputs. They write code. They solve hard problems. They bring medicine, law, finance, language, and science into the work. They help models get better.

The AI work economy is still fragmented. What people know is scattered across Reddit, Discord, WhatsApp, forums, private groups, and memory. A contractor in Nairobi, a reviewer in São Paulo, a coding expert in Warsaw, and a researcher in Toronto may be looking at the same platforms and still cannot see the same picture.

Happy Tasking exists to organize that knowledge — so the people doing the work, and the companies that need their expertise, can meet in a market that is easier to understand.

---

Before spending hours on an assessment, joining a platform, accepting an opportunity, learning a new skill, or committing professional time, a contributor should be able to ask ordinary questions and get a serious answer:

- What does this company actually offer?
- What do contributors report about it?
- What rates are people seeing?
- Is work currently available?
- How stable are opportunities?
- How does the company handle problems?
- What skills are currently valuable?
- Where does my professional profile fit?

That is the meaning of **Know before you task.**

The problem is not “workers versus companies.” The problem is information asymmetry. AI companies and contributors need each other. One side has projects, capital, and demand. The other has judgment, craft, and time. Both are weaker when the market is opaque.

---

## Experience becomes knowledge.

One person knows something about pay. Another about onboarding. Another about whether tasks are flowing this week. Another about reviewer quality, screening, or what happened when a payment was late.

Individually, those are anecdotes. They are still true. They are not yet useful to the next person making a decision.

Structured with care, and aggregated across a community, they can become intelligence:

**Experience → Structured signal → Community knowledge → Better decisions**

Happy Tasking is that conversion, done in public, with the limits that professionalism requires.

---

## Share your experience, not confidential work.

This is a hard line, not a slogan.

Happy Tasking wants contributors to share their **experience**: how a platform treated them, what they were paid, whether work was available, whether a dispute was handled fairly. It does not exist to expose confidential projects, client identities, private prompts, task answers, internal guidelines, proprietary assessments, reviewer identities, private company communications, or anything a legitimate confidentiality obligation protects.

> Share your experience, not confidential work.

Transparency and confidentiality are not opposites. You can say that onboarding was slow without pasting the exam. You can say pay arrived late without naming the end client. You can describe a reviewer’s fairness without identifying the reviewer. The work itself stays where it belongs.

A community that leaks confidential material is not brave. It is unusable — for contributors who would be fired, and for companies that would never engage with it.

---

## Reputation should be earned.

Independent reputation is a public good. It is not a product feature that a customer can purchase.

Advertising, sponsorships, recruiting relationships, research contracts, or any other commercial relationship must not modify TaskScore, TaskRate, TaskPulse, Resolution Score, or independent community reviews.

Happy Tasking may work commercially with companies. Understanding a workforce, hiring with consent, or buying research is legitimate. Changing what the community reported is not.

> Companies may pay Happy Tasking to understand the workforce. They cannot pay Happy Tasking to change what the workforce says.

If those two activities ever share a ledger, independence is already gone.

---

## Problems deserve resolution, not just complaints.

Every large platform generates problems. Pay questions, access issues, unclear guidelines, sudden dry spells. The interesting question is not “does this company receive complaints?” It is **what happens when something goes wrong.**

The Resolution Center is for legitimate issues, reported in public-safe terms, and for companies that will stand behind a verified profile and answer. Accountability should notice resolution: speed, seriousness, whether the same failure repeats.

Complaint without reply is incomplete. Reply without change is incomplete. A market that can see both is fairer than a market that only hears the loudest thread on Discord.

---

## Matching should work both ways.

Traditional recruiting asks: are you a fit for this opportunity?

Happy Tasking also asks: is this opportunity a fit for you?

**YOU → ROLE** is skills, experience, location, language, availability, and professional interest.

**ROLE → YOU** is independent community intelligence: pay, stability, reputation, availability, and how problems get resolved.

TaskMatch exists to help people decide, not to maximize applications. A match that sends someone into a dry queue or a poor-fit assessment is not a success. A match that helps someone walk away is doing its job.

---

## Your data should work for you.

A professional profile is first a tool for the person who built it. Skills and experience are there to improve TaskMatch, personalize market context, surface relevant opportunities, and show where a skill is thin.

Aggregate, de-identified information may help explain workforce trends. That is a public benefit. It is not a license to publish a private résumé.

Private professional information should not become public by default. Recruiter access to identifiable contributor information should require an explicit choice. If data only creates value for a buyer, the profile is facing the wrong direction.

---

## Built with the community.

Happy Tasking is community-driven. The community platform is open source under Apache-2.0.

Developers and domain experts can inspect the code, report bugs, propose features, discuss how metrics are built, improve accessibility and translations, submit pull requests, and challenge assumptions. How to work in the repository is in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). How decisions are made is in [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md). Where the product is heading is in [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md).

Open source is not a substitute for judgment. It is a way to keep judgment inspectable.

---

## Transparency includes methodology.

TaskScore, TaskRate, TaskPulse, Resolution Score, and TaskMatch should not arrive as mysterious numbers. Where we can, we show methodology, sample size, freshness, confidence, and what kind of source produced the signal.

A number without context can mislead. A high score on five reviews is not the same object as a high score on five hundred. Last week’s availability is not last year’s. DEMO fixtures are not community observation. If we cannot yet say how a figure was made, we should not pretend it is more than it is.

---

## Workers and companies are not enemies.

A healthy AI-work ecosystem needs contributors who are treated fairly **and** companies that can find qualified expertise without burning months of screening.

Happy Tasking should help both sides.

For contributors: better information, better matching, better preparation, better decisions.

For companies: better feedback, better resolution, a clearer picture of the workforce, and — where people opt in — better access to qualified talent.

The goal is a more efficient and transparent market, not a moral victory for one side of a contract.

---

## Independence matters.

Happy Tasking should be willing to show good experiences and bad ones, improving trends and declining ones, high pay and low pay, high availability and empty queues.

The platform does not exist to praise companies. It does not exist to attack them. It exists to measure and explain the AI-work economy as accurately as the available data allows — and to say clearly when the data is thin.

Independence is a practice: what we refuse to sell, what we refuse to hide, and what we refuse to invent.

---

## The flywheel.

More contributors  
↓  
More structured experience  
↓  
Better community intelligence  
↓  
Better TaskMatch  
↓  
Better decisions  
↓  
More useful Happy Tasking  
↓  
More contributors

Growth that spends trust is not growth. A larger community that leaks confidential work, sells its scores, or blurs DEMO with observation would be a worse map than the scattered chats we started with. The flywheel only turns if people still believe the picture.

# Built by AI workers, for AI workers.

AI work is becoming a global labor market. The people in that market — trainers, developers, reviewers, annotators, researchers, clinicians, lawyers, linguists, contractors, and the companies that hire them — deserve visibility into it.

Where the work is. What it pays. What people experience. Which skills matter. Which opportunities fit. What happens when things go wrong.

Happy Tasking exists to make that information easier to see, understand, and improve.

Built with the community.

Independent by design.

Know before you task.
